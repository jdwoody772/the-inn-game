# The Inn — Narrative Design Bible (through Scene 23)
Status: narrative reference for development. Established story beats are distinguished from tentative wording and future plans.

## Narrative principle
The game is not a chain of disconnected puzzle rooms. Room geometry, interactions, environmental clues, combat, and recovered abilities must carry the story. The player discovers Luca's past alongside Luca. Preserve mystery and gradual realization; do not explain revelations prematurely.

## Luca's arc
Luca awakens with incomplete memory and lost werewolf powers. His journey is both recovery of identity and a revenge story. The first phase builds toward confronting the Emperor, who sought to extract Luca's powers. Revenge and catharsis are the intended direction; do not invent a branching unity-versus-revenge choice.

## Early-game setup
- Opening room appears to be an inn. Luca finds a dagger on the floor (+1 STR in existing design); blood is visible near the fireplace.
- These details are planted evidence, not arbitrary decoration: the dagger was used against Luca during the extraction/attempted killing, and the room is actually a ceremonial chamber.
- A surviving werewolf pinned beneath a cart recognizes Luca as having returned, before Luca understands his identity.
- In town, humans can treat Luca as one of their own while displaying a dead werewolf as a trophy. This introduces discomfort and the first seeds of belonging to the persecuted werewolves.
- The mill werewolf boss indicates Luca must recover his abilities if he is to defeat the Emperor. His recovered abilities are pieces of his stolen werewolf essence, not merely game upgrades.

## Scene 17 — slaughtered den
Luca discovers a slaughtered werewolf den. One survivor urges him to recover his abilities and avenge them. The setting itself should communicate devastation before dialogue does. This is a strong escalation of responsibility, not yet the complete reveal.

## Scene 19 — ruins and historical discovery
- Entry puzzle: move a human statue and a werewolf statue until their hands meet; this unlocks the ruins. The mechanical action embodies their historical cooperation.
- Floor placards are arranged in a circle, like historical museum inscriptions.
- Placard 1: werewolves chose friendship with humans.
- Placard 2: werewolves protected humans.
- Placard 3: humans gave werewolves refuge.
- Placard 4: deliberately smashed; Luca/player reconstructs it to read that humans and werewolves were to live together in peace for eternity (exact wording not finalized).
- Crucial interpretation: the fourth placard was ALREADY broken by someone, presumably human. The player does not choose to break it. The reconstruction is an act of discovery and evidence of a violated peace, not a moral-choice mechanic.
- This is a discovery beat, not Luca's final turning point.

## Scene 20
Primarily traversal, enemies, items, and scenery. Preserve pacing and continuity; specific details remain implementation-dependent.

## Scene 21 — pounce recovery
Luca traverses ascending platforms to reach and regain Pounce. The skill permits a second jump; pressing B a third time during the sequence triggers a steep downward strike. In current design the strike uses a handheld arrow with bow equipped or the equipped melee weapon otherwise, adding +50% STR to that attack. The ability is another recovered part of Luca's werewolf nature. Keep input rules and room layout aligned with current implementation.

## Scene 23 — broken bridge and ceremonial guard
- Luca must use Pounce to cross a chasm and encounter a high-ranking human ceremonial guard subordinate to the Emperor (not the Emperor).
- The guard is shocked Luca survived: 'You were supposed to be dead.' Seeing the stone dagger recovered in the woods, he demands it: 'Is that the stone dagger? Give it to me!' and attacks.
- The stone dagger bears an inscription invoking the werewolves' protection of the people; exact inscription is not final.
- After defeat, the guard retreats on the floor in fear while Luca advances and demands: 'Tell me who I am.'
- The guard reveals that Luca WAS a werewolf and that the humans attempted to extract his powers for the Emperor. They left him for dead when werewolves came to rescue him; those rescuers did not survive.
- Luca asks about the inn where he awoke. The guard says it was not an inn but the ceremonial room where the extraction was attempted.
- This retroactively transforms the opening scene: the blood and dropped dagger are evidence of the violence committed against Luca. This is the major story turn through Scene 23.
- The guard's frightened, stammering retreat and the revelation's pacing are essential. Avoid turning the reveal into a generic exposition dump.

## Direction beyond Scene 23
Luca progresses toward the Emperor's palace entrance. Before confronting the Emperor in this first phase, he must recover the ability to fully transform into a werewolf. The dramatic payoff is revenge and recovery of self. Exact subsequent rooms and ending remain to be designed.

## Development rules for Worker
1. Read this file before changing story scenes, dialogue, major props, abilities, or progression.
2. Preserve existing game mechanics and canonical dialogue where implemented; consult actual scene code before editing.
3. Do not overwrite subtle environmental clues with explicit early explanations.
4. Tie new puzzles to narrative or character development wherever possible.
5. Treat tentative wording as editable, but preserve the underlying facts and reveal order.
6. If code contradicts this reference, flag the discrepancy rather than silently rewriting canon.

## Visual and gameplay reference — The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past
Use *The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past* (SNES) as a major reference point for visual presentation, readable top-down environments, exploration, puzzle design, combat pacing, and environmental storytelling. Study how its world layout, secrets, landmarks, and dungeon progression make discovery feel rewarding. Apply those principles to *The Inn* while respecting its Game Boy-style technical and visual constraints, existing mechanics, original characters, and darker werewolf-revenge narrative. This is inspiration, not a request to copy Nintendo's art, maps, characters, music, or exact puzzles.
